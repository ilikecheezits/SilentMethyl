\begin{abstract}
DNA methylation is a key component of epigenetic regulation, yet directly measuring how individual nucleotide variants alter local methylation remains challenging due to limited matched genetic and epigenetic data. Here, we developed SilentMethyl, a multimodal gated-fusion framework that integrates a DNABERT-2 encoder for 1,000-bp CpG-centered DNA sequences with an MLP that encodes breast epigenomic context, including chromatin accessibility, histone modifications, and evolutionary conservation. Learned gates combine sequence and contextual representations to predict CpG methylation, while paired wild-type and mutated sequences are used to estimate variant-associated methylation changes.

Using chromosome-level data splitting, SilentMethyl was evaluated on 26,570 CpGs from held-out chromosomes. The fusion model achieved a beta-value MAE of 0.0993 and ROC-AUC of 0.9680, outperforming both sequence-only and context-only models. The benefit of incorporating epigenomic context varied across regulatory environments, with the largest improvements observed at CpG shores and regions with high ATAC-seq accessibility or H3K27ac signal. 

In 81 independent breast mQTL associations, predicted variant-induced methylation changes correlated with observed ALT-dosage effects (Spearman $\rho$ = 0.610), reproduced 79.0\% of effect directions, and achieved an AUROC of 0.872 for effect direction, although absolute effect magnitudes were not accurately captured. As a focused application, SilentMethyl was applied to 440 TCGA-BRCA synonymous SNV-CpG pairs to prioritize variants with potential methylation effects. Predicted perturbations were larger for variants in promoter/TSS and UTR regions than in gene bodies and decreased with increasing variant-to-CpG distance. The highest-ranked candidate, an NCOA2 synonymous variant located 9 bp from its paired CpG, showed a predicted hypomethylating shift of -0.1798 and exceeded all matched background variants. These findings demonstrate that integrating genomic sequence with epigenomic context can improve CpG methylation prediction and provide a practical framework for linking single-nucleotide variation to local epigenetic regulation, thereby prioritizing variants for downstream functional and experimental investigation.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

DNA methylation is a widely studied epigenetic modification involved in gene regulation, shown to be associated with genomic context, chromatin organization, and transcription \cite{jones2012functions,schubeler2015function}, and frequently dysregulated in disease. Single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) can influence local epigenetic states, but validating their direct regulatory effects is difficult because matched genetic and epigenetic data are limited. This motivates modeling DNA methylation to identify potentially functional variants.

Existing deep learning methods have been able to predict DNA methylation and other similar regulatory measures using local DNA sequence, neighboring methylation, and long-range sequence context\cite{angermueller2017deepcpg,zhou2015predicting,avsec2021effective}. While DNA sequence itself contains some motifs and local regulatory patterns, sequence alone misses the epigenetic context in which they operate. Reference epigenomic data, including chromatin accessibility, histone data, and evolutionary conservation, can account for some of this missing information. Predicting methylation at a single locus and estimating the effect of a base change on methylation are also distinct tasks. A model that accurately predicts baseline methylation may still estimate variant-induced changes poorly.

To address this, several frameworks for evaluating paired sequence changes already exist. CpGenie predicts CpG methylation and validates REF-ALT sequence changes against mQTLs\cite{zeng2017cpgenie}. INTERACT focuses on methylation in brain tissue, DeepMethylation/DDM combines DNA sequence with reference tissue data, and Metheven combines sequence with ATAC-seq chromatin accessibility\cite{zhou2022interact,li2026deepmethylation,liu2025methven}.

However, many of these existing approaches face important roadblocks. Existing studies generally quantify the contribution of sequence and epigenomic inputs only through aggregate model-performance differences. Previous works also fail to account for reverse-complement equivariance, leaving them blind to this important biological phenomenon\cite{mallet2021rc}. SilentMethyl was designed to address these gaps. Our framework uses gated fusion to show the relative importance of sequence and context information for individual probes. The model also enforces reverse complement stability through random orientation during training.

SilentMethyl also uses synonymous mutations as a focused baseline for variant prioritization, since they modify a base pair without changing the encoded amino acid. However, sSNVs can still affect local regulatory function, including splice recognition, RNA structure, transcript stability, translation, and regulatory sequences in exons. \cite{sauna2011understanding,stergachis2013exonic}.
Several existing studies already link known synonymous variants with DNA regulatory features. A synonymous \textit{MLH1} c.27G$>$A allele was found in individuals with constitutional \textit{MLH1} methylation, linked to Lynch syndrome\cite{alvarez2025mlh1}. Synonymous variants in \textit{TP53} with altered CpG content are linked to intragenic demethylation, leading to the usage of an alternative promoter\cite{blackburn2016tp53}. Synonymous \textit{ADAMTS13} variants can also alter molecular properties through the creation and destruction of CpG sites\cite{jankowska2022adamts13}. These examples highlight sSNVs as strong targets for model experimentation and discovery. 

Because these studies are done on varying tissue types, model targets, sequence window lengths, data splits, and more, their metrics do not provide a common benchmark for this study. To create a reliable independent analysis, SilentMethyl uses chromosome split data to train a transformer sequence model combined with a context MLP for gated fusion. The framework also predicts WT-MUT methylation changes and validates them using external mQTL associations. Moreover, SilentMethyl is used as a variant-prioritization framework to rank new variants. In short, SilentMethyl provides a multimodal framework for predicting local CpG methylation and estimating single-nucleotide variant--associated methylation changes in breast tissue, enabling the prioritization of variants with potential epigenetic effects.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data creation}
Methylation beta labels were constructed from the TCGA-BRCA HM450 matrix \cite{tcga2012breast,grossman2016gdc}. Using a sample type-11 filter, 97 solid-tissue-normal columns were selected from 893 total. We checked that these 97 columns represented 97 unique participants without duplicate samples. 

We represented single probe methylation as the median of patient beta values. For CpG $i$, if $O_i$ represents the set of patients with a recorded methylation measurement at CpG $i$, then 

{\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{4pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{4pt}
\setlength{\abovedisplayshortskip}{4pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayshortskip}{4pt}
\begin{gather*}
\beta_i = \operatorname{median}_{s\in O_i}\beta_{i,s},
\qquad y_i = \mathbf{1}[\beta_i>0.5],\\[1ex]
\widetilde{\beta}_i =
\max\left(10^{-4},\min\left(1-10^{-4},\beta_i\right)\right),\\[1ex]
M_i=\log_2\left(\frac{\widetilde{\beta}_i}
{1-\widetilde{\beta}_i}\right).
\end{gather*}}
The transformation from beta to M-values follows \cite{du2010betamvalue}.

All probes required an exact CpG centered DNA sequence and a valid reference context window. None of the 418,486 probes in our combined dataset were flagged by HM450's \texttt{MASK\_general} filter. Median CpG probe coverage in our dataset was 97 out of 97 total, and 95.1\% of loci had at least 78 valid patients. Probe coordinates and DNA sequence were then pulled from the hg38 HM450 annotation resource \cite{zhou2017infinium,chen2013crossreactive}.

The data were split by chromosome into training, validation, and test sets before model training. Chromosomes 10--11 formed the validation set, chromosomes 8--9 formed the test set, and the remaining chromosomes formed the training set (Table~\ref{tab:splits}). By separating data by chromosome, we ensured no sequence windows overlapped between training, validation, and testing sets.

Each DNA input comprised a 1,000-bp CpG-centered sequence. The target CpG nucleotides were located at 499 and 500(zero-indexed). The epigenetic input comprised seven MCF-10A attributes, including ATAC-seq chromatin accessibility and six histone marks(H3K4me3, H3K27ac, H3K27me3, H3K9me3, H3K36me3, H3K4me1), averaged across a CpG centered 100 bp sequence. Breast context also contained two PhyloP scores for the two nucleotides in the center CpG. A missingness mask was assigned to each feature. Missing values were imputed using the corresponding feature's global median. The reference data were derived from shared resources and were not patient-specific.

\subsection{Models, training, and evaluation}
The sequence module was made up of a pretrained DNABERT-2(117M) transformer\cite{zhou2024dnabert2}, followed by a convolutional layer. Then, masked attention pooling was applied:
\[
\alpha_t=\frac{\exp(s_t)}{\sum_{u\in V}\exp(s_u)},\qquad
\mathbf h_{\rm seq}=\sum_{t\in V}\alpha_t\mathbf e_t,
\]
where $V$ contains non-padding positions and $\mathbf{e_i}$ denotes the embedding at position $i$. This produced a 768-dimensional vector $\mathbf h_{\rm seq}$.

The context component consisted of an MLP that mapped the 18 variable epigenetic reference input (9 features + 9 missingness masks) into  a 768-dimensional vector $\mathbf h_{\rm ctx}$.

After LayerNorm was applied on $\mathbf h_{\rm seq}, \mathbf h_{\rm ctx}$ to produce $\widetilde{\mathbf h}_{\rm seq}, \widetilde{\mathbf h}_{\rm ctx}$, they were then concatenated into a 1536-dimensional vector $[\widetilde{\mathbf h}_{\rm seq}; \widetilde{\mathbf h}_{\rm ctx}]$. This was then fed through a gating MLP producing  two final outputs $g_{\rm seq}$ and $g_{\rm ctx}$.
\newline
The final 768-dimensional global embedding was then calculated as:
\[
\mathbf z=g_{\rm seq}\widetilde{\mathbf h}_{\rm seq}
          +g_{\rm ctx}\widetilde{\mathbf h}_{\rm ctx}.
\]
\newline
$\mathbf z$ was then fed into two prediction head MLPs with the same architecture. 
The regression predicted $M$ value, which was converted to $\beta$ value by $\widehat\beta=\frac{2^{\widehat M}}{1+2^{\widehat M}}$. The classification head predicted $\widehat{y} = \mathbf 1[\sigma(\text{logit})>0.5]$. 
The training objective was the sum of Huber loss ($\delta=1.345$) on the regression head and binary cross-entropy on the classification head.  

Sequence and context models were trained independently with AdamW, with DNABERT-2 fully fine-tuned. Sequence, context, and fusion training used up to 10, 15, and 10 epochs and learning rates of $2\times10^{-5}$, $10^{-3}$, and $5\times10^{-4}$, respectively. During fusion training, the component encoders were frozen and only the normalization, gating, and prediction modules were updated.

During training, forward or reverse-complement DNA orientation was chosen with equal probability for each probe. For each model seed of 42, 43, and 44,the checkpoint model was chosen by lowest validation beta MAE. Training runs used seeds 42, 43, and 44. Primary prediction metrics included RMSE and MAE for both $\beta$ and $M$ values for regression, and ROC-AUC for classification. 

To control for similarities in nearby prediction errors, we split the test set into 257 non-overlapping blocks. We then created 5000 new test sets by resampling regions with replacement, 257 times per set. Sequence to fusion and context to fusion differences across error metrics were calculated across these test sets. This allowed for the construction of 95\% confidence intervals to check for consistent improvement across local regions. 

We also conducted a post-validation analysis, where CpG probes in testing data were grouped by GENCODE category and by ATAC-seq and H3K27ac quartiles. We measured the difference between sequence and fusion $\beta$ MAE in each group. Confidence intervals were constructed in a similar manner to regional CpG prediction sensitivity, using the same 257 blocks and 2000 new test sets created from resampling. Prioritized synonymous variants were then studied based on the groups their CpG probes belonged in, along with CpG--Variant distance.

\subsection{Paired sequence scoring and sSNV construction}

For each SNV, predicted beta methylation difference was calculated and averaged across forward and reverse complement strands: 
\[
\Delta\widehat\beta=
\frac{(\widehat\beta_{\rm MUT, FWD}-\widehat\beta_{\rm WT, FWD})+
(\widehat\beta_{\rm MUT, RC}-\widehat\beta_{\rm WT, RC})}{2}
\]
where $\rm MUT$ and $\rm WT$ represent the mutated and wild-type DNA input sequences, respectively. $\Delta\widehat M$ is defined similarly.

Tagged somatic sSNVs were obtained through the GDC and amino acid preservation was verified through hg38 and GENCODE v44 \cite{grossman2016gdc,frankish2021gencode}. Variants were then matched with the closest existing HM450 CpG probe. 440 unique variants remained after filtering out variants with probes flagged by \texttt{MASK\_general} and outside the 1000 bp window of their CpG. Variants were prioritized by $|\Delta\widehat\beta|$, predicted as the ensemble average of seed 42, 43, and 44 fusion models. For each variant, we recorded $\Delta\widehat\beta$ signs across seeds, FWD-RC prediction difference, and relative rank against other synonymous variants with similar mutation context and distance to CpG. 

\subsection{External mQTL analyses}

Significant eGTEx Breast Mammary Tissue lead mQTLs ($q<0.05$) were overlapped with CpGs in our test set \cite{oliva2023mqtl}. After excluding variants outside the DNA sequence window or altering their target CpG, 81 unique mQTL associations with 70 unique variants remained. Predicted variant ALT-REF methylation changes were compared against mQTL allele slopes.

Signed Spearman correlation measured correlation between the relative ordering of methylation change and mQTL slope, while absolute Spearman correlation determined an association between their relative magnitudes. Sign agreement and AUROC showed the model's ability to separate positive and negative mQTL slopes. Another CpG-variant sensitivity analysis dropped mutations within or immediately adjacent to a HM450 probe.

We then tested if predicted difference magnitude separated significant lead associations from nonsignificant high-$q$ associations ($q\geq0.5$). Significant and high-$q$ variants were located on the same chromosome and had minor allele frequency differences of no more than 0.05. 35 pairs were matched from 53 significant and 51 high-$q$ variants. We then used AUROC and average precision to test if $|\Delta\widehat\beta|$ and $|\Delta\widehat M|$ were able to separate the two groups. 

\section{Results}

\subsection{Multimodal fusion outperforms other models on methylation prediction}

Results were obtained on the 26,570-probe test set. The fusion model had the strongest performance across all evaluation metrics and seeds. This consistent trend suggests that DNA sequence and epigenomic context complement each other in predicting DNA methylation.

The lightweight context MLP established a good baseline beta MAE of $0.1395\pm0.0001$ and ROC-AUC $0.9187\pm0.0001$. Replacing the context model with the sequence model reduced beta MAE by $21.2\%$($0.1099 \pm 0.0008$), while increasing ROC-AUC to $0.9569\pm0.0007$. However, the best results came from the fusion model, which further improved beta MAE by $9.6\%$($0.0993 \pm 0.0025$) from the sequence model and raised ROC-AUC to $0.9680\pm0.0021$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:performance}B, \ref{fig:performance}C). Predictions remained stable across probes with lower coverage. On probes with measurements from at least 78 of 97 patients, the seed 42 fusion model achieved a beta MAE of 0.09669 and ROC-AUC of 0.97066. The locus-specific fusion gates also indicate the relative contributions of the two modalities.

The fusion model's improvement in test metrics was consistent across multiple training seeds. Fusion improved sequence-only MAE by 0.01043 (95\% CI 0.00951--0.01132) and ROC-AUC by 0.01053 (95\% CI 0.00909--0.01207), with confidence intervals constructed as described in Section 2.2. Therefore, the fusion architecture showed success in using multimodal input data to make consistently stronger improvements in both classification and regression tasks, compared to baseline models.

\subsection{Reference context contributes across a variety of regulatory conditions}

Stratified biological and epigenomic groups showed certain patterns in sequence-to-fusion improvement. Fusion reduced beta MAE across every stratum in GENCODE region, CpG island status, ATAC-seq, and H3K27ac signal. By GENCODE annotation, beta MAE reduction ranged from 0.0088 at intergenic probes to 0.0130 at UTR locations. By CpG island status, the largest improvement came from shores(0.0162 beta MAE reduction) (Fig.~\ref{fig:context}A). Fusion also lowered beta MAE by 0.0139 in the highest ATAC-seq quartile (Fig.~\ref{fig:context}B) and 0.0147 in the highest H3K27ac quartile (Fig.~\ref{fig:context}C).

These results show that the benefits of incorporating reference context into fusion are not random. Reference information may have provided useful complementary evidence at CpG shores and areas of high ATAC-seq and H3K27ac signal, whereas the sequence model may have struggled more in making accurate predictions there.

To test if SilentMethyl's predicted variant-associated methylation differences agreed with external data, we evaluated the three-seed fusion ensemble on 81 independent breast mQTL associations from 70 unique variants. SilentMethyl correctly predicted 64 of 81 reported effect directions (79.0\%), achieved signed Spearman $\rho=0.610$, and successfully separated positive from negative mQTL slopes with an AUROC of 0.872 (Fig.~\ref{fig:mqtl_combined_rank}A). This pattern was still robust after removing all sSNVs on or directly adjacent to a HM450 CpG probe. Across the remaining 53 mQTL associations, the fusion ensemble achieved a signed Spearman $\rho=0.610$, 75.5\% direction agreement, and an AUROC of 0.872. These results demonstrate that the model's signed REF-ALT difference predictions are consistent with observations in the external breast-tissue validation set.

However, SilentMethyl remains limited in accurately predicting absolute effect magnitudes and distinguishing significant mQTL leads. While the model's signed predictions were highly consistent with signed mQTL data, the association between absolute predicted and observed effects was weak with $\rho = 0.217$ on the full mQTL set (Fig.~\ref{fig:mqtl_combined_rank}B) and $\rho = 0.282$ after HM450 variant filtering. In addition, the fusion model was unable to separate matched significant mQTL leads vs. high-$q$ leads based on $|\Delta\widehat M|$, based on an AUROC of 0.512 with a 95\% CI of 0.380--0.646.

In summary, the mQTL analysis showed that SilentMethyl's fusion model predictions agreed with observed breast mQTL relative rankings and effect directions, although its capacity to accurately predict absolute effect magnitudes and separate matched significant leads remains limited.

Having shown the consistency of SilentMethyl's ranked responses with an external validation dataset, we then used the fusion model as a prioritization engine for 440 synonymous variants--CpG pairs.
Cross-seed ranking correlations ranging from r=0.681--0.699 demonstrated rank consistency in this new dataset. Fusion and sequence-only rankings were also highly concordant (signed $\rho=0.981$, absolute $\rho=0.966$, and 96.6\% sign agreement). This suggests that predicted methylation responses to single-nucleotide changes were driven mainly by sequence information.

We stratified variants by their genomic region to study association with predicted methylation difference (Fig.~\ref{fig:candidate-context}A). The median $\vert{}\Delta\widehat\beta\vert{}$ was 0.00360 for promoter/annotated TSS variants,  0.00261 for UTR variants, and 0.00165 for gene-body variants. Strata based on variant-to-CpG distance showed even clearer differences (Fig.~\ref{fig:candidate-context}B). Variants within 50 bp of assigned CpG loci had median $\vert{}\Delta\widehat\beta\vert{}=0.00512$, while variants 251--500 bp away had median $0.00111$.
In summary, the model tended to predict larger methylation changes in promoter/annotated TSS and UTR regions than in gene bodies, despite genomic regions not being provided model features. Predicted differences also typically decreased as variant--CpG distance increased. 


\subsection{Biological Characterization of a Prioritized \textit{NCOA2} Variant.}

Here, we use our TCGA dataset's top synonymous variant as an example of how an observed somatic change can be transformed into a biological hypothesis. chr8:g.70148394G$>$A in \textit{NCOA2} is a synonymous GGC$>$GGT substitution on transcript ENST00000452400.7. The variant is 9 bp from probe cg20699548 and located in the Gene Body. The probe cg20699548 is in an OpenSea relation and is in the 4th quartile for both ATAC-seq and H3K27ac signal compared to our test data. Here, our fusion ensemble model predicted a large hypomethylating shift of $\Delta\widehat\beta=-0.1798\pm0.0198$.

The \textit{NCOA2} candidate also had an extreme $|\Delta\widehat\beta|$ when compared with a group of similar synonymous variants. Its absolute predicted response exceeded those of all 66 variants selected for the matched-background comparison (Fig.~\ref{fig:top-candidate}). This example shows how SilentMethyl can prioritize single-nucleotide variants against background mutations using predicted methylation differences.

Prioritized variants and associated genes can be investigated further to develop testable hypotheses. \textit{NCOA2} encodes a nuclear receptor coactivator, and previous work reported increased expression of the gene in some breast tumors and reduced breast cancer growth after NCOA2 depletion \cite{cai2019ncoa2}. Previous literature does not directly support a causal effect between chr8:g.70148394G$>$A and increased NCOA2 activation, but our model predicts substantial demethylation at cg20699548 due to this variant. This supports the hypothesis that chr8:g.70148394G$>$A may alter regulation in \textit{NCOA2} due to the demethylation of cg20699548. Future experiments could measure the causal methylation change due to this variant and assess how it affects \textit{NCOA2} expression and breast cancer behavior.

\subsection{Evaluating \textit{STK11} as a nonsynonymous case study}
While SilentMethyl's prioritization engine focused on somatic synonymous variants, our framework extends to any kind of single nucleotide variant. This can simply be done by modifying any base pair in the mutated sequence from the wild type sequence while context remains fixed, regardless if the change is synonymous or not. Then, WT-MUT model differences can be calculated and ranked. As an example, we evaluated a group of studied nonsynonymous SNVs in breast cancer-related genes. The two highest-ranked variants were both located in \textit{STK11}, a gene known to be associated with hereditary breast cancer risk in Peutz--Jeghers syndrome \cite{lipsa2019stk11}.

The highest predicted variant, rs2145420809, produced a predicted shift of $\Delta\widehat\beta = 0.0774$ at probe cg16601904, being immediately adjacent to its probe. This mutation converted an encoded asparagine into a serine and is in the gene body. Its corresponding CpG, cg16601904, is located at a CpG shelf and is in the 2nd quartile of ATAC-seq and 3rd quartile for H3K27ac signal. The next highest predicted mutation was rs148928808, which had a change of $\Delta\widehat\beta = -0.0613$ at probe cg08681293, despite being 204 bp downstream of the CpG. This mutation converted a tyrosine into a stop codon and is in the gene body as well. Its corresponding CpG, cg08681293, is a CpG shore and in the 3rd quartile of ATAC-seq and 2nd quartile of H3K27ac signal. Both variants are on transcript NM\_000455.5.

While biological assessment of rs2145420809 is still uncertain, rs148928808 is a known pathogenic variant associated with hereditary cancer-predisposing syndrome and Peutz-Jeghers syndrome. However, SilentMethyl's difference estimates indicate large methylation shifts at both probes due to their variants. This analysis shows how SilentMethyl can evaluate clinical variants and generate hypotheses that prioritize variants on the basis of predicted WT--MUT methylation differences.

\section{Discussion}

SilentMethyl establishes a unified framework for two complementary tasks in regulatory genomics: predicting baseline CpG methylation and estimating how that methylation state responds to single-nucleotide variation. Across held-out chromosomes, integrating local DNA sequence with breast epigenomic context consistently improved methylation prediction over either modality alone, demonstrating that sequence and regulatory context provide complementary information about CpG state. At the same time, paired WT--MUT scoring enabled the same framework to move beyond locus-level prediction and prioritize individual variants according to their predicted effects on local methylation. Together, these results connect baseline epigenetic state prediction with variant-level regulatory interpretation within a single modeling framework.

The contribution of epigenomic context was particularly informative in regulatory environments where sequence alone was less sufficient. Fusion produced the largest gains at CpG shores and at loci with high ATAC-seq accessibility or H3K27ac signal. CpG shores frequently exhibit tissue- and disease-associated methylation variation, while chromatin accessibility and H3K27ac are hallmarks of active regulatory regions\cite{jones2012functions,schubeler2015function,encode2012integrated}. The preferential improvement at these loci therefore suggests that methylation in regulatory-active regions reflects an interaction between local sequence and chromatin state rather than sequence information alone. This genomic-context dependence provides biological support for the multimodal architecture: the added epigenomic features do not simply improve average prediction accuracy, but contribute most strongly in regions where regulatory state is expected to carry additional information.

A distinct strength of SilentMethyl is that its variant-response predictions can be evaluated independently of baseline methylation performance. In breast mQTL data, predicted ALT--REF methylation changes recovered 79.0\% of observed effect directions, correlated with reported effects in signed rank (Spearman $\rho=0.610$), and distinguished positive from negative mQTL effects with an AUROC of 0.872. mQTLs provide population-level evidence that genetic variation is associated with DNA methylation \cite{oliva2023mqtl}, making this agreement an important external validation of the paired-sequence perturbation strategy. By contrast, absolute predicted response magnitudes showed substantially weaker correspondence with observed mQTL effect sizes. SilentMethyl therefore appears most informative for identifying the direction and relative priority of methylation-responsive variants rather than for reproducing their exact biological effect sizes. This distinction defines a practical use of the model: ranking variants for downstream investigation rather than attempting to replace direct molecular measurement.

The predicted variant effects also exhibited clear genomic organization. Among the 440 synonymous SNV--CpG pairs, variants in promoter/TSS regions produced larger methylation shifts than variants in UTRs or gene bodies, and response magnitude declined markedly as variant--CpG distance increased. Promoter-proximal methylation is closely connected to transcriptional regulation \cite{jones2012functions,schubeler2015function}, while local genetic effects on CpG methylation are known to depend strongly on genomic proximity, a principle incorporated into previous sequence-based methylation-effect models \cite{zeng2017cpgenie,liu2025methven}. The emergence of both regional and distance-dependent patterns in SilentMethyl predictions indicates that the learned sequence representation captures spatial features relevant to local methylation regulation. In particular, the substantially larger responses for variants within 50 bp of the target CpG suggest that the model is most sensitive to nucleotide changes occurring within the immediate regulatory neighborhood of the methylation site.

The \textit{NCOA2} analysis demonstrates how these predictions can be translated into a specific biological hypothesis. The highest-ranked synonymous candidate, chr8:g.70148394G$>$A, lies only 9 bp from cg20699548 and occurs in a local context with high ATAC-seq and H3K27ac signals. Its predicted hypomethylating effect exceeded those of all 66 matched synonymous background variants. \textit{NCOA2} encodes a nuclear receptor coactivator implicated in breast cancer growth and MAPK/ERK signaling, and experimental depletion of \textit{NCOA2} has been reported to suppress breast cancer cell proliferation \cite{cai2019ncoa2}. The combination of a large predicted methylation response, close CpG proximity, active regulatory context, and established disease relevance therefore prioritizes this variant--CpG pair for targeted functional investigation, including direct measurement of cg20699548 methylation and \textit{NCOA2} expression following sequence perturbation.

The \textit{STK11} analysis further expands the scope of the framework beyond synonymous variation. SilentMethyl identified substantial predicted methylation responses for two nonsynonymous \textit{STK11} variants, including rs148928808, a pathogenic truncating variant associated with Peutz--Jeghers syndrome and hereditary cancer predisposition\cite{lipsa2019stk11}. These variants have biological consequences independent of methylation, but their analysis illustrates an additional application of SilentMethyl: clinically relevant variants can be interrogated for local epigenetic responses alongside their established coding consequences. The framework is therefore not intrinsically restricted to synonymous SNVs; synonymous variants provide a useful focused application because they preserve amino-acid sequence, whereas nonsynonymous and noncoding variants broaden the model toward more general variant-associated methylation analysis.

Several limitations define the current scope of these findings. Methylation targets were derived from cohort medians of 97 TCGA solid-tissue-normal breast samples collected adjacent to tumors and may therefore retain field effects or cell-composition heterogeneity \cite{tcga2012breast,teschendorff2016field}. The epigenomic context was derived from MCF-10A reference tracks rather than matched individual samples, and HM450 measurements cover only a subset of genomic CpGs. Evaluation was also performed using one chromosome-holdout design, and the external mQTL benchmark represents germline variation whereas the TCGA prioritization analysis includes somatic variants. These limitations motivate validation in independent breast cohorts, broader CpG coverage, patient- or cell-type-specific epigenomic data, additional genomic and tissue settings, and ultimately direct experimental testing of highly ranked SNV--CpG pairs.

Overall, SilentMethyl links three levels of analysis that are often considered separately: local methylation state, regulatory genomic context, and nucleotide-level perturbation. Multimodal integration improves CpG methylation prediction where regulatory context is most informative, independent mQTLs support the direction and relative ordering of predicted variant responses, and genomic stratification and targeted case studies connect these predictions to interpretable biological features. This framework therefore provides a scalable route from sequence variation to experimentally testable hypotheses about local epigenetic regulation and offers a foundation for extending variant-effect prediction across broader classes of genetic variation and biological contexts.

\section{Conclusion}
In summary, SilentMethyl is capable of predicting methylation along with paired differences caused by a single variant in unseen chromosome regions. Fusion outperformed sequence-only and context-only baselines for single-probe prediction. Predicted mutation responses from the sequence baseline and fusion model agreed with independent mQTL data in terms of direction and relative ordering. Finally, SilentMethyl was able to rank 440 distinct synonymous variants by prediction difference, creating hypotheses of regulatory significance to be tested experimentally.
