\begin{abstract}
\color{addedred}
DNA methylation is shaped both by local sequence and by the chromatin context in
which that sequence sits, but establishing which single-nucleotide variants
actually change methylation remains difficult. We present SilentMethyl, a gated
fusion of a pretrained DNA language model with reference epigenomic features that
predicts CpG methylation and the methylation change a variant causes. Under
chromosome-blocked splitting the model reaches a $\beta$-value MAE of 0.0993 and
ROC-AUC of 0.968 on 26{,}570 held-out CpGs, outperforming six comparators
including the CpGenie and DeepCpG architectures retrained on identical data.
Predicted variant effects replicate across two independent cohorts --- 66{,}495
variant--CpG pairs from an African American whole-blood meQTL study and 76{,}893
from tissue-matched breast --- agreeing in direction and rank, surviving matching
on variant--CpG distance, and pooling with no detectable heterogeneity. Two
findings generalise beyond this model. Gated fusion improves absolute prediction
but contributes nothing to variant-effect prediction, because context features
are identical for both alleles and therefore cannot create a variant effect; and
uncertainty on bounded bimodal targets is confounded by range compression, so
calibration must be assessed in logit space. Predicted effect magnitude does not
by itself identify clinically annotated variants.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

DNA methylation is a widely studied epigenetic modification involved in gene regulation, shown to be associated with genomic context, chromatin organization, and transcription \cite{jones2012functions,schubeler2015function}, and frequently dysregulated in disease. Single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) can influence local epigenetic states, but validating their direct regulatory effects is difficult because matched genetic and epigenetic data are limited. This motivates modeling DNA methylation to identify potentially functional variants.

Existing deep learning methods have been able to predict DNA methylation and other similar regulatory measures using local DNA sequence, neighboring methylation, and long-range sequence context\cite{angermueller2017deepcpg,zhou2015predicting,avsec2021effective}. While DNA sequence itself contains some motifs and local regulatory patterns, sequence alone misses the epigenetic context in which they operate. Reference epigenomic data, including chromatin accessibility, histone data, and evolutionary conservation, can account for some of this missing information. Predicting methylation at a single locus and estimating the effect of a base change on methylation are also distinct tasks. A model that accurately predicts baseline methylation may still estimate variant-induced changes poorly.

To address this, several frameworks for evaluating paired sequence changes already exist. CpGenie predicts CpG methylation and validates REF-ALT sequence changes against mQTLs\cite{zeng2017cpgenie}. INTERACT focuses on methylation in brain tissue, DeepMethylation/DDM combines DNA sequence with reference tissue data, and Metheven combines sequence with ATAC-seq chromatin accessibility\cite{zhou2022interact,li2026deepmethylation,liu2025methven}.

{\color{addedred}
Long-context models such as Enformer and AlphaGenome predict many regulatory
modalities but not CpG methylation directly
\cite{avsec2021effective,avsec2025alphagenome}, whereas MethylGPT operates on
measured methylation profiles and cannot score an unobserved substitution
\cite{ying2024methylgpt}. Sequence-based methylation-effect prediction therefore
remains a distinct problem.
}

However, many of these existing approaches face important roadblocks. Existing studies generally quantify the contribution of sequence and epigenomic inputs only through aggregate model-performance differences. Previous works also fail to account for reverse-complement equivariance, leaving them blind to this important biological phenomenon\cite{mallet2021rc}. SilentMethyl was designed to address these gaps. Our framework uses gated fusion to show the relative importance of sequence and context information for individual probes. The model also enforces reverse complement stability through random orientation during training.

SilentMethyl also uses synonymous mutations as a focused baseline for variant prioritization, since they modify a base pair without changing the encoded amino acid. However, sSNVs can still affect local regulatory function, including splice recognition, RNA structure, transcript stability, translation, and regulatory sequences in exons. \cite{sauna2011understanding,stergachis2013exonic}.
Reported synonymous variants in \textit{MLH1}, \textit{TP53}, and
\textit{ADAMTS13} have been linked to altered methylation or CpG content
\cite{alvarez2025mlh1,blackburn2016tp53,jankowska2022adamts13}, motivating their
use as focused targets for model experimentation.

Because prior studies differ in tissue, targets, sequence windows, and data
splits, their metrics are not directly comparable. SilentMethyl instead uses
chromosome-held-out data to train a gated sequence--context model, validates
paired WT--MUT predictions against external mQTLs, and ranks variants with
potential local methylation effects.

{\color{addedred}
The expanded evaluation tests 66{,}495 held-out GENOA pairs alongside
tissue-matched eGTEx data and uses distance matching, block resampling,
published-model comparisons, and motif and clinical controls to define the
limits of variant-response ranking.
}

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data construction}
Methylation beta labels were constructed from the TCGA-BRCA HM450
matrix \cite{tcga2012breast,grossman2016gdc}. Using a sample-type-11 filter, 97
solid-tissue-normal columns were selected from 893 total, verified to represent
97 unique participants without duplicate samples.

Single-probe methylation was represented as the median of patient beta values.
For CpG $i$, if $O_i$ is the set of patients with a recorded measurement at CpG
$i$, then

{\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{4pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{4pt}
\setlength{\abovedisplayshortskip}{4pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayshortskip}{4pt}
\begin{gather*}
\beta_i = \operatorname{median}_{s\in O_i}\beta_{i,s},
\qquad y_i = \mathbf{1}[\beta_i>0.5],\\[1ex]
\widetilde{\beta}_i =
\max\left(10^{-4},\min\left(1-10^{-4},\beta_i\right)\right),\\[1ex]
M_i=\log_2\left(\frac{\widetilde{\beta}_i}{1-\widetilde{\beta}_i}\right).
\end{gather*}}
The beta-to-$M$ transformation follows \cite{du2010betamvalue}.

All probes required an exact CpG-centered DNA sequence and a valid reference
context window. None of the 418{,}486 probes in the combined dataset were flagged
by the HM450 \texttt{MASK\_general} filter. Median probe coverage was 97 of 97,
and 95.1\% of loci had at least 78 valid patients. Probe coordinates and sequence
were taken from the hg38 HM450 annotation
resource \cite{zhou2017infinium,chen2013crossreactive}.

Data were split by chromosome before training: chromosomes 10--11 for validation,
chromosomes 8--9 for test, remaining chromosomes for training
(Supplementary Table~1). Splitting by chromosome ensures no sequence windows
overlap between partitions.

Each DNA input was a 1{,}000-bp CpG-centered sequence with the target CpG
nucleotides at zero-indexed positions 499 and 500. The epigenetic input comprised
seven MCF-10A attributes---ATAC-seq accessibility and six histone marks
(H3K4me3, H3K27ac, H3K27me3, H3K9me3, H3K36me3, H3K4me1)---averaged across a
CpG-centered 100-bp window, plus two PhyloP scores for the two nucleotides of the
central CpG. A missingness mask accompanied each feature; missing values were
imputed with the feature's global median. Reference data were derived from shared
resources and were not patient-specific.

\subsection{Models, training, and evaluation}

Figure~\ref{fig:silentmethyl_workflow} summarises target construction, the
chromosome partition, the gated architecture, and the paired scoring and
evaluation paths described in this section.
The sequence module comprised a pretrained DNABERT-2 (117M)
transformer \cite{zhou2024dnabert2} followed by a convolutional layer and masked
attention pooling:
\[
\alpha_t=\frac{\exp(s_t)}{\sum_{u\in V}\exp(s_u)},\qquad
\mathbf h_{\rm seq}=\sum_{t\in V}\alpha_t\mathbf e_t,
\]
where $V$ contains non-padding positions and $\mathbf{e}_t$ is the embedding at
position $t$, producing a 768-dimensional $\mathbf h_{\rm seq}$.

The context module was an MLP mapping the 18-dimensional epigenetic input
(9 features $+$ 9 missingness masks) to a 768-dimensional
$\mathbf h_{\rm ctx}$.

After LayerNorm produced $\widetilde{\mathbf h}_{\rm seq}$ and
$\widetilde{\mathbf h}_{\rm ctx}$, the two were concatenated and passed through a
gating MLP yielding scalars $g_{\rm seq}$ and $g_{\rm ctx}$. The fused embedding
was
\[
\mathbf z=g_{\rm seq}\widetilde{\mathbf h}_{\rm seq}
          +g_{\rm ctx}\widetilde{\mathbf h}_{\rm ctx},
\]
fed to two prediction heads. The regression head predicted $M$, converted by
$\widehat\beta=2^{\widehat M}/(1+2^{\widehat M})$; the classification head
predicted $\widehat{y}=\mathbf 1[\sigma(\text{logit})>0.5]$. The objective was
Huber loss ($\delta=1.345$) on regression plus binary cross-entropy on
classification.

Sequence and context models were trained independently with AdamW, with DNABERT-2
fully fine-tuned. Sequence, context, and fusion training used up to 10, 15, and 10
epochs at learning rates $2\times10^{-5}$, $10^{-3}$, and $5\times10^{-4}$.
During fusion training the component encoders were frozen and only normalization,
gating, and prediction modules were updated.

During training, forward or reverse-complement orientation was chosen with equal
probability per probe. For seeds 42, 43, and 44 the checkpoint was selected by
lowest validation beta MAE. Primary metrics were RMSE and MAE for $\beta$ and
$M$, and ROC-AUC for classification.

The trained models were then used for paired sequence scoring. For each SNV,
the predicted methylation difference was averaged across strands:
\[
\Delta\widehat\beta=
\frac{(\widehat\beta_{\rm MUT, FWD}-\widehat\beta_{\rm WT, FWD})+
(\widehat\beta_{\rm MUT, RC}-\widehat\beta_{\rm WT, RC})}{2},
\]
with $\Delta\widehat M$ defined analogously. Because the reported prediction is
$\tfrac12[f(x)+f(\mathrm{RC}(x))]$, it is exactly invariant under reverse
complementation.

Tagged somatic sSNVs were obtained through the GDC with amino-acid preservation
verified against hg38 and GENCODE
v44 \cite{grossman2016gdc,frankish2021gencode}. Variants were matched to the
nearest HM450 probe; 440 unique variants remained after removing
\texttt{MASK\_general}-flagged probes and variants outside the 1{,}000-bp window.
Variants were prioritized by $|\Delta\widehat\beta|$ from the three-seed fusion
ensemble.

{\color{addedred}
After model evaluation, we grouped the held-out CpGs by biological context. Each
CpG was assigned to one GENCODE region category in the following order:
promoter/TSS, UTR, gene body, or intergenic. CpGs were also grouped by their
relation to CpG islands and by ATAC and H3K27ac signal quartiles. Within each
group, we measured the benefit of reference context as the sequence-only beta
MAE minus the fusion beta MAE, so a positive value indicated better fusion
performance. Confidence intervals were estimated using 2,000 resamples of
complete 1-Mb genomic regions. Candidate variants were separately grouped by
genomic region and distance from the paired CpG. These groupings were used only
to interpret model behavior and did not affect training, model selection, or
candidate ranking.
}

\subsection{External variant evaluation and statistical analysis}
\label{sec:genoa-methods}
{\color{addedred}

External validation used GENOA cis-meQTL summary statistics from an African
American whole-blood cohort \cite{shang2023genoa}. SNP positions were converted
from hg19 to hg38, 0.1\% of allele-inconsistent pairs were discarded, and effects
reported per minor allele \cite{gemma2012} were re-signed to the model's
REF$\rightarrow$ALT direction. The independent held-out stratum contained
66{,}495 pairs (39{,}657 variants and 19{,}081 probes). Variants outside the
window or at the target CpG were excluded; primary results use the 42{,}866 pairs
that neither create nor destroy a CpG.

\label{sec:stats}

Confidence intervals resampled complete 1-Mb blocks to preserve local dependence.
Because significant GENOA pairs were closer to their CpGs than null pairs
(median 191 versus 258\,bp), each significant pair was matched without
replacement to a null at the same distance. Distance therefore had AUROC 0.500
in the matched cohort. Model differences were evaluated on one shared matched
cohort with paired block bootstrap intervals.

The complete eGTEx association set provided a secondary cohort-wide analysis.
After allele alignment and sequence-window filtering, 76{,}893 held-out
variant--CpG pairs remained; 418 significant non-CpG-altering pairs met the
study's two-stage permutation threshold and formed the primary tissue-matched
stratum. Signed correlation, direction agreement, calibration slope, and AUROC
were evaluated with 1-Mb block resampling.

Significant eGTEx Breast Mammary Tissue lead mQTLs ($q<0.05$) were intersected
with CpGs on the held-out test chromosomes \cite{oliva2023mqtl}. Variants outside
the 1,000-bp sequence window or altering the target CpG were excluded, leaving 81
associations from 70 unique variants. Predicted ALT-minus-REF methylation changes
were compared with reported ALT-dosage slopes. Signed Spearman correlation
measured agreement in the relative ordering of signed responses, sign concordance
measured the fraction of predictions with the correct direction, and AUROC
measured separation of positive and negative slopes. Absolute Spearman
correlation assessed whether predicted and observed response magnitudes had
similar ordering. A probe-overlap sensitivity analysis additionally excluded
variants within the annotated rHM450 probe span or at either immediately
adjacent nucleotide.

We also tested whether predicted response magnitude distinguished significant
mQTLs from nonsignificant high-$q$ leads ($q\geq0.5$). Significant and high-$q$
variants were matched one-to-one on the same chromosome, with an MAF difference
no greater than 0.05 and preference for similar variant--CpG distance, baseline
methylation, and substitution type. The procedure produced 35 pairs from pools
of 53 significant and 51 high-$q$ variants. AUROC and average precision tested
whether $|\Delta\widehat M|$ or $|\Delta\widehat\beta|$ separated the two groups.
Pair resampling produced confidence intervals, and within-pair label
permutations produced $p$-values.
}

\subsection{Additional analyses and controls}
\label{sec:uncertainty-methods}
{\color{addedred}

Forward/reverse-complement disagreement was compared with three-seed variability,
the boundary heuristic $-|\widehat\beta-0.5|$, and a random control using
risk--coverage curves. Analyses were repeated on the unbounded $M$ scale and
within predicted-$\beta$ strata to distinguish uncertainty signal from the
mechanical compression of error near 0 and 1.

\label{sec:tumor-methods}

The shifted-target analysis compared fixed predictions with median methylation
from 699 TCGA-BRCA primary tumours whose participants were absent from the 97
normal samples; all 791 tumour samples are reported in the Supplement. Because
the sequence and MCF-10A inputs do not change, this tests degradation after
tumour-associated target shifts, not generalisation to new tumour inputs.

\label{sec:motif-methods}

JASPAR CORE vertebrate motifs \cite{castrocolin2013jaspar} were scanned on both
strands across the 42{,}866 GENOA pairs. Mutant scores were evaluated at the same
offset and strand as the best wild-type occurrence. Per-factor Spearman
correlations between motif-score change and $\Delta\widehat M$ were
Benjamini--Hochberg corrected; composition, distance, substitution, and motif
redundancy controls are detailed in the Supplementary Methods.

\label{sec:published-methods}

CpGenie \cite{zeng2017cpgenie} and DeepCpG
\cite{angermueller2017deepcpg} were reimplemented from released source and
trained on the same splits, windows, augmentation, loss, and validation-selected
grids. DeepCpG used its DNA module because measured neighbouring methylation is
allele-invariant. Architecture and tuning details are in the Supplement.

\label{sec:matched-methods}

A pre-registered test asked whether predicted effect magnitude is enriched for
disease-associated variants. Among held-out pairs with a measured methylation
effect ($p<0.05$), variants were labelled by GWAS Catalog overlap on rsID or
exact coordinate, then matched to unlabelled pairs on variant--CpG distance and
allele frequency. The probe was the independent unit and intervals are 1\,Mb
block bootstraps; pre-registration, matching balance, and the power calculation
are in the Supplementary Methods.
}

\section{Results}

\subsection{Multimodal fusion outperforms single-modality models}
\label{sec:performance}

Results were obtained on the 26,570-probe test set. The fusion model had the strongest performance across all evaluation metrics and seeds. This consistent trend suggests that DNA sequence and epigenomic context complement each other in predicting DNA methylation.

The context MLP achieved beta MAE $0.1395\pm0.0001$ and ROC-AUC
$0.9187\pm0.0001$. Sequence reduced beta MAE by $21.2\%$ to
$0.1099\pm0.0008$ and increased ROC-AUC to $0.9569\pm0.0007$. Fusion further
reduced beta MAE by $9.6\%$ to $0.0993\pm0.0025$ and reached ROC-AUC
$0.9680\pm0.0021$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:performance}).

Across seeds, fusion improved sequence-only MAE by 0.01043 (95\% CI
0.00951--0.01132) and ROC-AUC by 0.01053 (0.00909--0.01207).

{\color{addedred}
A ridge model over 2{,}772 reverse-complement-collapsed $k$-mers matched the
context model's ROC-AUC, but fusion reduced its beta MAE by 36.6\%
(Table~\ref{tab:performance}). Only four held-out windows had a nearest-training
31-mer Jaccard score above 0.5, and excluding \texttt{MASK\_snp5\_common} probes left fusion beta MAE unchanged
while slightly raising ROC-AUC, arguing against composition, near-duplicate
memorization, and genotype artefact.
}

\subsection{Reference context across regulatory conditions}

Fusion reduced beta MAE in every GENCODE, CpG-island, ATAC-seq, and H3K27ac
stratum. Gains ranged from 0.0088 at intergenic probes to 0.0130 at UTRs and
were largest at CpG shores (0.0162), in the highest ATAC-seq quartile (0.0139),
and in the highest H3K27ac quartile (0.0147; Supplementary Figure~1). This
concentration in regulatory-active loci supports a complementary contribution
from reference context.



\label{sec:tumor-results}
{\color{addedred}
Against median methylation from 699 primary tumours, fixed fusion predictions
worsened from beta MAE 0.0975 to 0.1180. Two-thirds of probes shifted by less
than 0.05, and error rose mainly at the largest target shifts. This is a
shifted-target analysis, not evaluation on tumour-specific inputs; the lack of
comparable context-only degradation remains unexplained.
}

\subsection{External mQTL validation across cohorts}
\label{sec:genoa-results}
\color{addedred}

The harmonized eGTEx breast analysis contained 76{,}893 held-out pairs. Among
418 significant non-CpG-altering pairs, fusion reached signed $\rho=0.246$
(0.114--0.402) and 59.6\% direction agreement (Table~\ref{tab:cohorts}). The focused set of 81 published lead mQTLs reached $\rho=0.610$ (0.452--0.731),
but is reported separately because lead variants are enriched for larger, more
precisely measured effects. On that set predicted sign separated positive from
negative reported slopes at AUC 0.872 (0.780--0.947), unchanged when variants
inside the annotated probe span were excluded (AUC 0.877, 54 associations).
Magnitude ordering was weaker than sign: absolute predicted change correlated
with absolute reported slope at only $\rho=0.217$ ($-0.014$--$0.421$).

Predicted magnitude did not, however, separate significant eGTEx leads from
matched nonsignificant ones: across 35 matched sets AUROC was 0.512
(0.380--0.646, permutation $p=0.43$). Sign is recovered where an association
exists; magnitude alone does not flag which leads are significant.

Among 4{,}037 genome-wide significant non-CpG-altering GENOA pairs, fusion
achieved signed $\rho=0.152$ (0.117--0.188) and 55.3\% direction agreement
(Table~\ref{tab:genoa}). Agreement was weaker than in breast but remained above
chance despite differences in tissue and ancestry.

\color{black}
\subsection{Distance-aware discrimination and model comparison}
\color{addedred}

Distance was a major confound: it alone separated significant from null GENOA
pairs at AUROC 0.595 (0.583--0.609), similar to the model's unmatched AUROC of
0.600 (0.586--0.614). After matching every significant pair to a null pair at
the same distance, fusion retained AUROC 0.570 (0.554--0.585) and remained above
chance within each distance bin (Fig.~\ref{fig:discrimination}). By contrast, the
position-blind $k$-mer ridge fell to AUROC 0.503 (0.488--0.518), despite a signed
correlation of 0.100 with measured effects. SilentMethyl therefore captures a
modest positional signal beyond proximity and sequence composition; its larger
unmatched AUROC should not be interpreted independently of distance.

\color{black}
\subsection{Mechanistic interpretation, uncertainty, and motif controls}
\label{sec:equivalence}
\color{addedred}

Fusion substantially improved baseline methylation but not paired variant
effects. Fusion-minus-sequence differences were $+0.0026$
($-0.0020$--$+0.0070$) for signed $\rho$, $-0.0007$
($-0.0064$--$+0.0051$) for direction agreement, and $-0.0021$
($-0.0053$--$+0.0011$) for distance-matched AUROC. These narrow intervals bound
any improvement to less than 0.007 and support practical equivalence.

This follows from the architecture. The context vector depends on the probe, not
on the allele, and is therefore identical for wild-type and mutant input. Writing
the fused embedding difference,
\[
\Delta\mathbf z=
\big[\widetilde{\mathbf h}^{\rm mut}_{\rm seq}g^{\rm mut}_{\rm seq}
-\widetilde{\mathbf h}^{\rm wt}_{\rm seq}g^{\rm wt}_{\rm seq}\big]
+\widetilde{\mathbf h}_{\rm ctx}\big[g^{\rm mut}_{\rm ctx}-g^{\rm wt}_{\rm ctx}\big],
\]
the context term survives only through a gate shift that is itself driven by the
sequence change. Context can modulate a variant effect; it cannot create one.

Fusion-minus-sequence differences were also flat across gate-DNA-share
quartiles. This explains their agreement among the 440 synonymous candidates
($\rho=0.981$; 96.6\% sign agreement) and shows why paired-allele fusion models
with invariant covariates require a sequence-only ablation.

\label{sec:uncertainty-results}

Forward/reverse-complement disagreement provided a single-model uncertainty
signal, correlating with per-locus error at approximately $\rho=0.50$ and
retaining 75--83\% of the incremental information from three-seed variability.
However, a boundary heuristic, $-|\widehat\beta-0.5|$, appeared even stronger on
the beta scale because absolute beta error is compressed near 0 and 1. On the
unbounded $M$ scale, the heuristic's within-stratum correlation collapsed from
0.0369 to 0.0099, while cross-seed variability and orientation disagreement were
stable (Fig.~\ref{fig:uncertainty}). We therefore evaluate uncertainty in logit
space, where performance is less affected by the bounded target.

\label{sec:motif-results}

Across 831 motifs, the strongest couplings between motif-score change and
predicted methylation change shared the ETS core \texttt{GGAA}
(Supplementary Table~4; Fig.~\ref{fig:motifs}). Motif weakening accompanied
predicted hypermethylation, consistent with loss of protective binding. Yet the
$k$-mer ridge reproduced these couplings more strongly than the neural models
(ELF4 $-0.467$ versus $-0.266$). The association therefore demonstrates
sensitivity to \texttt{GGAA} composition, not learned binding-site grammar.

The two model classes differed elsewhere. In the motif-scanned cohort, the
$k$-mer model achieved meQTL-discrimination AUROC 0.516 (0.496--0.535), compared
with 0.592 for fusion. A position-blind count model can reproduce composition
effects but cannot represent where a substitution occurs relative to the CpG.
This separates the neural model's positional resolution from the motif
composition signal.

Disruption magnitude was unrelated to predicted effect ($\rho=-0.011$,
$-0.023$--$+0.002$), and strongly disrupting variants showed no greater meQTL
discrimination. GC, substitution, and global-offset controls are reported in the
Supplement.

\color{black}
\subsection{Synonymous variant prioritization and the \textit{NCOA2} case study}

{\color{addedred}
The synonymous screen uses held-out chromosomes, whereas the later
\textit{STK11} examples use training probes and illustrate only the scoring
workflow.
}

We ranked 440 synonymous variant--CpG pairs. Cross-seed rank correlations were
0.681--0.699, while fusion and sequence-only rankings were highly concordant
(signed $\rho=0.981$, absolute $\rho=0.966$, 96.6\% sign agreement), again
indicating that paired responses were driven mainly by sequence.

Median $|\Delta\widehat\beta|$ was 0.00360 for promoter/TSS, 0.00261 for UTR,
and 0.00165 for gene-body variants (Supplementary Figure~2A). Distance
showed a stronger gradient: the median fell from 0.00512 within 50\,bp to 0.00111
at 251--500\,bp (Supplementary Figure~2B).



{\color{addedred}
The highest-ranked synonymous candidate was chr8:g.70148394G$>$A in
\textit{NCOA2}, a GGC$>$GGT substitution on ENST00000452400.7. It lies in the
gene body, 9\,bp from OpenSea probe cg20699548, which is in the highest quartile
of both ATAC-seq and H3K27ac signal. The three-seed fusion ensemble predicted a
hypomethylating response of $\Delta\widehat\beta=-0.1798\pm0.0198$.

Its response exceeded all 66 matched synonymous variants
(Supplementary Figure~3). Because \textit{NCOA2} depletion reduces
breast-cancer cell proliferation \cite{cai2019ncoa2}, the pair motivates direct
measurement of cg20699548 methylation and \textit{NCOA2} expression, without
constituting a causal claim.
}



\subsection{Nonsynonymous extension and clinical interpretation}

{\color{addedred}
The same paired scoring procedure applies to nonsynonymous SNVs. In a screen of
reported variants in breast-cancer-related genes, the two largest predicted
responses were in \textit{STK11}, a gene associated with hereditary breast-cancer
risk in Peutz--Jeghers syndrome \cite{lipsa2019stk11}. rs2145420809
(p.Asn119Ser) was immediately adjacent to shelf probe cg16601904 and produced
$\Delta\widehat\beta=0.0774$; its probe was in the second ATAC-seq and third
H3K27ac quartiles. rs148928808 (p.Tyr292Ter) was 204\,bp from shore probe
cg08681293 and produced $\Delta\widehat\beta=-0.0613$; its probe was in the
third ATAC-seq and second H3K27ac quartiles. Both variants are in the gene body
on transcript NM\_000455.5.

The biological relevance of rs2145420809 remains uncertain, whereas
rs148928808 is a truncating variant reported in Peutz--Jeghers syndrome. In this
study, however, the examples are not independent validation: both target probes
belong to the training split. Their value is to demonstrate how nonsynonymous
variants can be placed within the complete scored background and selected for
follow-up (Fig.~\ref{fig:stk11-screen}).
}

{\color{addedred}
Predicted magnitude did not classify disease-associated variants.
\label{sec:translational}
Among held-out variants with a measured methylation effect ($p<0.05$), labelled
by GWAS Catalog overlap and matched on variant--CpG distance and allele
frequency, the GWAS share of the top 5\% of predicted effects did not differ
from chance in either cohort (eGTEx 0.490, 0.289--0.673, 510 labelled pairs;
GENOA 0.596, 0.417--0.752, 573). Both tests were powered to detect a moderate
enrichment and found none. SilentMethyl therefore ranks predicted methylation
consequence, not pathogenicity or disease risk.
}

\section{Discussion}

SilentMethyl establishes a unified framework for two complementary tasks in regulatory genomics: predicting baseline CpG methylation and estimating how that methylation state responds to single-nucleotide variation. Across held-out chromosomes, integrating local DNA sequence with breast epigenomic context consistently improved methylation prediction over either modality alone, demonstrating that sequence and regulatory context provide complementary information about CpG state.

At the same time, paired WT--MUT scoring enabled the same framework to move beyond locus-level prediction and prioritize individual variants according to their predicted effects on local methylation. Together, these results connect baseline epigenetic state prediction with variant-level regulatory interpretation within a single modeling framework.

Fusion produced the largest gains at CpG shores and at loci with high ATAC-seq
or H3K27ac signal. Shores often exhibit tissue- and disease-associated
methylation variation, while accessibility and H3K27ac mark active regulatory
regions \cite{jones2012functions,schubeler2015function,encode2012integrated}.
This concentration supports the view that reference context contributes most
where sequence alone is less sufficient.

{\color{addedred}
Predictions agreed modestly with measured effects across GENOA and eGTEx
(pooled $\rho=0.178$), and GENOA agreement returned to chance in the null
stratum. Because distance explained much of the unmatched discrimination, the
key residual result is AUROC 0.570 after exact matching. CpGenie, DeepCpG, and
SilentMethyl recovered similar residual signal, while the position-blind
$k$-mer baseline returned to chance; the evidence therefore supports positional
sequence-model sensitivity, not unique superiority of SilentMethyl.

Fusion improved baseline prediction but not paired effects because context was
identical for both alleles. Uncertainty was more meaningful on the unbounded
$M$ scale, and the $k$-mer reproduction of the ETS association limits its
interpretation to \texttt{GGAA} composition sensitivity.
}

{\color{addedred}
The held-out \textit{NCOA2} candidate combines a large response, close CpG
proximity, and active context into a testable hypothesis \cite{cai2019ncoa2}.
The training-split \textit{STK11} examples extend the workflow to nonsynonymous
variants but are not validation; neither case establishes causality without
direct perturbation.
}

Several limitations define the current scope of these findings. Methylation targets were derived from cohort medians of 97 TCGA solid-tissue-normal breast samples collected adjacent to tumors and may therefore retain field effects or cell-composition heterogeneity \cite{tcga2012breast,teschendorff2016field}. The epigenomic context was derived from MCF-10A reference tracks rather than matched individual samples, and HM450 measurements cover only a subset of genomic CpGs. Evaluation was also performed using one chromosome-holdout design, and the external mQTL benchmark represents germline variation whereas the TCGA prioritization analysis includes somatic variants. These limitations motivate validation in independent breast cohorts, broader CpG coverage, patient- or cell-type-specific epigenomic data, additional genomic and tissue settings, and ultimately direct experimental testing of highly ranked SNV--CpG pairs.

{\color{addedred}
GENOA differs in tissue and ancestry, normalized effects prevent direct
calibration, and motifs were computationally predicted. GWAS overlap is a noisy proxy for causality, since most catalog entries tag rather than cause an association. Direction agreement did not improve across quintiles of reference-effect
precision (0.521 to 0.566, intervals overlapping), so the ceiling reflects model
resolution rather than noise in the reported effects. Repeated chromosome partitions were not evaluated, so
intervals describe one held-out split.
}



\section{Conclusion}
In summary, SilentMethyl is capable of predicting methylation along with paired
differences caused by a single variant in unseen chromosome regions. Fusion
outperformed sequence-only and context-only baselines for single-probe
prediction{\color{addedred}, and also outperformed the CpGenie and DeepCpG
architectures retrained on identical data}. Predicted mutation responses from the
sequence baseline and fusion model agreed with independent mQTL data in terms of
direction and relative ordering{\color{addedred}, replicating in a
tissue-matched breast cohort and in a cross-tissue, cross-ancestry cohort with no
detectable heterogeneity}. Finally, SilentMethyl was able to rank 440 distinct
synonymous variants by prediction difference, creating hypotheses of regulatory
significance to be tested experimentally{\color{addedred}, while two
pre-registered tests establish that predicted magnitude alone does not identify
clinically annotated variants}.
